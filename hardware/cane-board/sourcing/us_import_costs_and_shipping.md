## 1. What US duties and tariffs apply on October 13, 2026 to China-origin bare PCBs, assembled boards and loose components?

### Takeaway
The stack on October 13, 2026 is: normal (MFN) duty + the legacy China Section 301 duty (25% on Lists 1–3, 7.5% on List 4A, 50% on semiconductors) + a new 12.5% Section 301 "forced labor" duty that began July 24, 2026; IEEPA tariffs ended February 24, 2026 and the 10% Section 122 surcharge expired July 24, 2026. In practice: bare PCBs 37.5%, most assembled boards 37.5%–41%, passives and connectors 37.5%, integrated circuits and diodes 50%, radio modules 7.5% or 25%. Chips, computer parts and telecom modules are exempt from the 12.5% layer.

### Inferences
- Rule of thumb for a duty estimate on China-origin goods today: bare PCB 37.5% (12.5% only if a still-valid 2-/4-layer exclusion is actually claimed on the entry); assembled board 37.5%–41% for most function-specific headings; resistors, capacitors, connectors 37.5%; ICs, diodes and probably transistors/LEDs 50%; Wi-Fi/Bluetooth modules 7.5% or 25%.
- The Section 232 semiconductor duty (9903.79.01) targets advanced data-centre-class chips with end-use carve-outs, so it should not apply to hobby microcontrollers, motor drivers or small assembled boards; the aggregator's 50% figure for 8473.30.11 probably over-counts for such goods.
- A motor-controller board like the cane board would most plausibly be entered under a control-apparatus or "parts of electrical machines" heading (37.5%–41%), not the computer-parts heading; the seller, not the buyer, picks the code on a DDP shipment.
- Even if a bare-board exclusion applies in law, the two sellers that publish DDP rates charge flat percentages (35% and 37.5%) that do not pass any exclusion through (see question 3).

### Gaps
- The Federal Register notice 2026-15181 and its annexes could not be read (bot block), and CBP's "Forced Labor HTS LIST" attachment is a PDF that was not downloaded; exemption status per HTS line therefore rests on a third-party aggregator and trade press.
- The exact text of U.S. note 20(vvv) for printed circuit boards (which layer counts and substrates, which 10-digit lines) was not found in any page opened; whether it is still in the 178 extended exclusions is asserted by Altium and NextPCB only.
- USITC's search output for 8534.00.00 showed no footnote linking it to 9903.88.03; the 25% Section 301 linkage comes from the aggregator and sellers.
- The aggregator (Wove) shows the Section 122 line ending on dates between July 9 and July 16, 2026 on different pages, which conflicts with the July 24 expiry in every other source; its other details may be similarly imprecise.
- No HTS-level source was found for transistors (8541.2x), LEDs (8541.41), crystals, inductors or modules other than 8517.62.
- Which 10-digit line a bare Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module falls in (hence 7.5% vs 25%) was not confirmed.

## 2. Is there any duty-free de minimis allowance for low-value parcels from China today?

### Takeaway
No. The $800 duty-free allowance ended for China and Hong Kong on May 2, 2025 and for every country on August 29, 2025, and it was kept suspended by Executive Order 14388 of February 20, 2026. Every commercial parcel from China now pays duty from the first dollar: courier parcels through an informal entry filed by the courier, and postal parcels (since July 24, 2026) at the normal HTS rates prepaid through the carrier instead of a flat postal rate.

### Cited Findings
- **[Third party]** China-origin shipments lost the exemption on May 2, 2025; the global suspension took effect August 29, 2025. — [Zonos tariff tracker](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/us-tariff-changes)
- **[Official]** Executive Order 14388, "Continuing the Suspension of Duty-Free De Minimis Treatment for All Countries", signed February 20, 2026, effective February 24, 2026; postal shipments were to pay a duty equal to the Section 122 surcharge rate, collected and remitted by carriers. — [govinfo DCPD-202600130 (via search snippet)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202600130/html/DCPD-202600130.htm); [Expeditors summary](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/continued-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment)
- **[Official]** CBP e-commerce FAQ (last modified September 2, 2026): the suspension "applies to merchandise valued at $800 or less arriving via all modes"; CBP calls it "the indefinite suspension of the de minimis exemption"; a new mail informal entry process "takes effect July 24, 2026"; "Duty rates are based on the HTSUS classification and the rates in effect at the time the entry is made." — [CBP e-commerce FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs)
- **[Official]** Same page: "Informal entry is generally allowed for shipments valued at $2,500 or less"; formal entry is required above $2,500; "Mail shipments are exempt from MPF" except Inbound Express Mail (EMS); exemptions for bona fide gifts and personal or household articles are unchanged, but a gift excludes purchases. — [CBP e-commerce FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs)
- **[Third party]** Postal history for Chinese-origin goods: 120% of value or $100 per item under IEEPA in 2025; reset to a flat 10% on February 24, 2026; the flat rate ended July 24, 2026 and postal shipments now pay standard duties (MFN, Section 232, Section 301); duties must be prepaid; the postal DDP threshold rose to $2,500 on July 24, 2026. — [Zonos tariff tracker](https://zonos.com/docs/guides/us-tariff-changes)
- **[Third party]** A 2025 law ends de minimis for all commercial shipments by statute from July 1, 2027. — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/de-minimis-exemption-changes-coming.html)
- **[Official, courier side]** CBP's merchandise processing fee for an informal entry rose to $2.77 per entry (from $2.69) on October 1, 2026 for fiscal year 2027; formal-entry minimum $34.58, maximum $670.86; dutiable mail fee $7.61. — [Livingston](https://www.livingstonintl.com/cbp-to-increase-user-fees-for-fiscal-year-2027-effective-october-1-2026/); [Juno Logistics](https://www.junologistics.com/customs-updates/us-customs-user-fees-to-increase-for-fiscal-year-2027)
- **Conflict [Seller]:** NextPCB's August 2026 article says "all shipments from China and Hong Kong require formal customs entry regardless of value" with an MPF minimum of "roughly $33.58 per entry"; CBP's FAQ says informal entry is generally allowed up to $2,500. — [NextPCB blog](https://www.nextpcb.com/blog/pcb-manufacturing-tariffs-2026-china-sourcing); [CBP e-commerce FAQs](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/e-commerce/faqs)
- **[Seller]** JLCPCB's carrier notes: DHL requires formal declaration above $800 declared value (written with reference to IEEPA); UPS uses informal clearance if the total is under $2,500 and each item under $250; FedEx needs a 10-digit HTS code on every US shipment. — [JLCPCB tariff FAQ](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/us-tariff-policy-faq)

### Inferences
- A $20–$200 parcel is far below the $2,500 informal-entry ceiling, so the realistic government charges are duty plus a $2.77 processing fee, not the ~$34 formal-entry minimum NextPCB cites.
- "Gift" marking does not help: the remaining gift exemption excludes purchases.
- Cheap "direct line" services that used to arrive without charges in 2025 (see question 6 user reports) can no longer rely on a flat postal rate; since July 24, 2026 the duty has to be prepaid at HTS rates by the line's operator, so it is either built into the shipping price or collected by the seller.

### Gaps
- The text of EO 14388 and CBP's own page on the July 24, 2026 mail process were not opened directly; details of postal collection come from Zonos.
- The dollar amounts of the remaining gift/personal exemptions are not stated on the CBP page read.
- One blog claims the allowance was "suspended indefinitely by CBP on 24 June 2026"; no official source for that date was found, so it is not relied on.

## 3. How do JLCPCB, PCBWay, LCSC, Seeed Studio, Elecrow, ALLPCB, PCBgogo and NextPCB handle US duties now?

### Takeaway
JLCPCB and PCBWay collect duty at checkout as a flat percentage of the goods (JLCPCB 35% for boards and assembly; PCBWay 37.5% for bare boards and 47.5% for assembly), and JLCPCB forces individuals onto these duty-paid express services. Seeed prepays duty at checkout on its China routes. LCSC, Elecrow, ALLPCB and PCBgogo publish only "buyer pays on delivery" terms, which means the courier bills duty plus its own fee; NextPCB advertises a DDP option but publishes no rate.

## 4. What do DHL Express, FedEx and UPS charge a US consumer when duty is collected on delivery?

### Takeaway
All three add a fee for advancing the duty with a minimum of about $17–$17.50 (2%–2.5% of the amount above that), and FedEx and UPS are reported to add a per-entry clearance charge of about $10–$20 on low-value parcels; the $2.77 CBP processing fee is passed through. On a $20–$200 order these fixed fees total roughly $20–$33, usually more than the duty.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official]** FedEx: effective July 20, 2026 the Disbursement Fee rose "from the greater of $15 or 2% of duty, tax, and merchandise processing fee charges" to "the greater of $17.50 or 2.5%". — [FedEx rate changes](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/rate-changes.html)
- **[Official]** FedEx: the "U.S. Inbound Processing Fee" is renamed "Inbound Processing Fee" from August 3, 2026 (amount not on the page); from January 18, 2027 a Residential Delivery Charge applies to qualifying international shipments to the US and a $25 Paper Document Fee is introduced. — [FedEx rate changes](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/rate-changes.html)
- **[Third party]** FedEx clearance entry fee: $9.75 for customs value up to $200 and $19.50 for $200.01–$800; worked example "$150 order with $15 in duty" comes to about $42 (duty $15, clearance entry $9.75, disbursement $15, MPF $2.69) — written before the July 20 increase. — [TariffsTool FedEx guide](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/fedex-customs-duty-bill-dispute)

- Working figures for a duty-unpaid parcel worth $20–$200 on October 13, 2026: DHL about $17.50 + $2.77; FedEx about $17.50 + $9.75 + $2.77 (if the clearance entry fee applies); UPS about $17.50 + ~$10 + $2.50 + $2.77 (if the entry preparation and processing fees apply to the lane). The percentage component only exceeds the minimum when duty passes $700–$875.
- A seller's DDP service replaces these consumer-side fees with whatever the seller builds in; on small orders DDP is therefore cheaper unless the seller's bundled fee exceeds about $20–$30.
- User reports show mis-assessment risk on duty-unpaid UPS parcels is real and hard to dispute; this is a non-price reason to prefer duty-paid checkout.

## 5. Is California sales or use tax collected on shipments to 95051, at what rate, and on top of duty?

### Takeaway
The City of Santa Clara rate is reported as 9.75% (7.25% state plus 2.5% district) following a county-wide rise on April 1, 2026. JLCPCB and LCSC both list California among the states where they collect sales tax at checkout, on goods plus shipping; whether a prepaid duty line is also taxed is not stated by either. Under California's own guidance, duty the buyer pays as importer is generally outside the tax base, while a tariff that is the seller's cost is inside it.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official]** CDTFA's current table is labelled "Tax Rates Effective October 1, 2026"; "The statewide tax rate is 7.25%"; district taxes range from 0.10% to 2.00% each (city rows are only in the PDF/Excel files, not read). — [CDTFA rates page](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/sales-use-tax-rates.htm)
- **[Third party]** Santa Clara County rate for unincorporated areas and cities without their own district tax rose from 9.125% to 9.750% on April 1, 2026 (CDTFA notice L-1022 cited); San Jose rose from 9.375% to 10.000%. — [TaxCloud](https://taxcloud.com/sales-tax-radar/2026-california-sales-tax-updates)
- **[Third party]** As of September 2026: City of Santa Clara 9.75% for most ZIP codes (7.25% state + 2.5% special district), with ZIP 95050 shown at 10%; Sunnyvale 9.75%; San Jose 10%. — [Kintsugi, Santa Clara County](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/california/santa-clara-county)

### Inferences
- For 95051 use 9.75% on goods plus shipping as the base case, and 9.75% on goods plus shipping plus prepaid duty as the upper case.
- In the user's order the tax equals 8.29% of goods plus shipping, 9.32% of goods alone, or 6.32% of everything; without the state none of these can be confirmed, so the example does not settle whether JLCPCB taxes the duty line.
- On a DDP shipment where the buyer remains importer of record (as Seeed states for its own), the CDTFA principle points to duty not being taxable, but a seller's tax engine may still tax it if it treats the line as part of the selling price.

Reading across the three: duty adds 35%–50% of goods value; a duty-unpaid express parcel then adds $20–$33 of fixed fees regardless of size; sales tax adds 9.75% of goods plus shipping. A quick landed-cost rule for a duty-paid checkout is: goods × (1 + duty rate) + shipping, then + 9.75% of goods and shipping.

### Gaps
- The base for each seller's percentage is confirmed only for JLCPCB (one user's order); PCBWay's base is assumed to be goods value.
- DHL's $17.50 comes from a search extract of DHL's page; the 2026 rate guide's shipper-billed option shows a $17.00 minimum. FedEx's $9.75 and UPS's $10 + $2.50 are third-party figures not confirmed for China-origin express parcels.
- LCSC's direct line: one December 2025 user paid nothing beyond shipping; no report after the July 24, 2026 postal change was found, so that route is not costed.
- Real checkout totals were not obtained (no carts, accounts or addresses were entered).

## 8. Do orders from US distributors (DigiKey, Mouser) or US board makers carry a tariff surcharge?

### Takeaway
Yes for the distributors: DigiKey and Mouser both pass US import tariffs through on affected parts shipped within the US, shown as a tariff charge on the order, and both updated their notices in 2026. No statement was found for US board makers.

### Cited Findings
- **[Seller]** DigiKey tariff resources page ("Last updated : September 8th, 2026"): explains tariff charges with the line "You received a Tariff product on your United States shipment"; state tax-exemption certificates "do not apply to Federal Tariff charges"; it uses "minimized tariff pass-through" together with its Foreign Trade Zone and drawback programs. — [DigiKey tariff resources](https://www.digikey.com/en/resources/tariff-resources)
- **[Seller]** DigiKey help page: the tariff "appears as a separate line item in your cart and on your invoice" and applies only to products with specific HTS codes, China as country of origin, consumed in the United States (page from 2025). — [DigiKey tariff information (via search snippet)](https://digikey.cn/zh/help/tariff-information)
- **[Seller]** DigiKey on 2026 changes: "IEEPA Tariffs (Reciprocal & Fentanyl) Ended" February 24, 2026; a "temporary Section 122 tariff of 10% on all imports into the United States for 150 days" began the same day; it "has already started the process of updating customer pricing"; on refunds, "Credits can only be issued if CBP refunds the duties" and no refund plan is feasible yet. The readable part of the page does not mention the July 24, 2026 change. — [DigiKey tariff resources](https://www.digikey.com/en/resources/tariff-resources)
- **[Seller]** Mouser: "effective September 1, 2025, Mouser will expand the application of tariff fees across our product offering"; it had worked since 2018 to absorb tariff costs; its notice records the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling and the 10% Section 122 duty of February 24, 2026, stacking on Section 301 and Section 232. — [Mouser tariff updates (via search snippet; page timed out)](https://www.mouser.com/tariff-updates/)
- **[Third party]** A law firm is soliciting Mouser customers over possible refunds of tariff fees tied to the invalidated IEEPA tariffs (a solicitation, not a neutral source). — [Chimicles](https://chimicles.com/mouser-electronics-tariff-surcharge-investigation/)
- **[Seller]** Seeed's US warehouse: prices rose 10–20% from April 25, 2025 because of duties, and about 26% of SKUs were reduced after November 10, 2025. — [Seeed, May 14, 2025 update (via search snippet)](https://www.seeedstudio.com/blog/2025/05/14/navigating-recent-u-s-tariff-adjustment-update/)

### Inferences
- Buying China-origin parts from a US distributor does not avoid the tariff; it converts it into a per-line surcharge on the distributor's (higher) price, with no courier advance fee, no customs processing fee and domestic shipping times. For non-China-origin parts the surcharge may be absent or smaller, since DigiKey's line item was tied to China origin.
- Because distributors import in bulk through a foreign trade zone, their tariff base is their import cost, not the retail price, so the surcharge as a share of the retail price can be lower than the 37.5%–50% a direct import pays.

### Gaps
- Neither distributor's current per-part tariff percentages nor a post-July 24, 2026 statement were found; Mouser's page could not be opened.
- No tariff statement from any US board fabricator (for example OSH Park) was found; whether US-made boards carry a materials surcharge is unknown.
- Whether DigiKey and Mouser include the tariff line in the California sales-tax base was not found.
